# Registered research protocol (draft; not preregistered externally)

## Primary question
How do identified exogenous tax-policy shocks and government-demand shocks affect employment, real wages and investment over 0–5 years? Does baseline AI exposure moderate the responses?

## Identification hierarchy
1. Narrative exogenous shocks with documentary justification and numeric replication series (US Romer/Romer, UK Cloyne, Canada Hussain).
2. Quasi-experimental geographic or industry variation with verified treatment assignment.
3. Observational panels, explicitly exploratory only.

## Predeclared outcomes
Employment-to-population ratio, payroll employment, hours worked, real compensation, real output, investment, labor share, household consumption, fiscal cost, and inflation. Prefer quarterly frequency for shock analysis; annual cross-country comparisons separately.

## Controls and robustness
Lagged shocks and outcomes, business-cycle states, pretrends, anticipation windows, alternate lag lengths, HAC bandwidth, outcome transformations, alternative controls, data vintages, placebo leads, and independent reproduction. Report multiple-testing corrections for hypothesis families. Do not interpret non-rejection of pretrend tests as proof of identification.

## AI interaction caution
Historic 1945–2010 shocks cannot directly identify modern generative-AI policy interactions. Later AI adoption measures must have explicit coverage and comparable definitions. Use forward-looking scenario modeling separately from causal estimates.

## Policy interpretation
No 'jobs created' attribution without counterfactual, cost basis, horizon, uncertainty and displacement/leakage accounting. Never equate GDP growth with employment or welfare. Record country institutions, inflation and distribution.
